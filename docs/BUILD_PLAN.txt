0. THE PRODUCT AND THE RESEARCH CLAIM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Real-life problem
  Much of what makes Bangladeshi destinations worth visiting travels by word of
  mouth: the kotkoti shops by the Mahasthangarh gate, the local name for a
  quieter stretch of beach, the ghat where you catch the boat, the season when
  a haor is worth the trip. None of it is organised anywhere. Official lists
  cover the famous sites; maps miss informal places; tourists crowd the same
  few spots; tourism money stays in a few hotels.

The product
  A platform that mines tourist reviews, vlogs and blogs (Bangla, Banglish and
  English) for mentions of lesser-known places, foods, activities, routes and
  tips. It resolves each mention to a real location, scores how "hidden" it
  is, and routes it through human verification before anything is published.
  Every claim links back to the texts that support it.

  Two faces, one evidence base:
    * Government / district officers: a district-by-district register of
      undocumented attractions, visitor complaints, trends, PDF briefs, and a
      verification workflow.
    * Tourists: the existing chat, itinerary and comparison tools, now surfacing
      verified gems next to famous places.

The research claim (one sentence)
  "Topic-level long-tail detection fails on small, translated corpora.
  Entity-level extraction from the original-language text recovers the
  lesser-known places and local knowledge it misses, and does so traceably."

  This builds directly on the current work. The existing long-tail detector
  found 0 long-tail preferences, and this plan turns that negative result into
  the motivation for the new contribution.

Evidence we already have (probe run on the 538 current reviews)
  Even a crude pattern match finds about 50 secondary places mentioned inside
  reviews of other places:
    - Fatrar Chor, Chor Bijoy, Gangamati Reserved Forest (Kuakata reviews)
    - Chandranath Temple, "Muradpur Beach, as it is locally known" (Guliakhali)
    - Madhabpur Lake (Srimangal); Boga Lake (Nilachal); Sardar Bari (Panam City)
  It also finds access knowledge: "get off at Fokirhat Bazar and take a CNG",
  "engine boat from Mongla Ferry Ghat along the Pashur River".
  Spelling variants of one place already appear: Guliakhali / Guliyakhali /
  Gulyakhali.

3. PHASE 2 — DATA COLLECTION AT NATIONAL SCALE (weeks 2-8)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Principle: hidden gems are mentioned inside reviews of famous places. Collect
where the mentions are, then follow them (snowball).

3.1 Anchor places: about 50 total, 5-8 per division, all 8 divisions.
    Starting list (verify each on Google Maps; replace any with few reviews):
      Dhaka:      Lalbagh Fort, Ahsan Manzil, Panam City/Sonargaon,
                  National Botanical Garden, Bhawal National Park
      Chattogram: Cox's Bazar, Saint Martin's, Bandarban (Nilgiri, Nilachal),
                  Rangamati/Kaptai, Sitakunda, Khagrachari (Alutila)
      Sylhet:     Jaflong, Ratargul, Bisnakandi, Lalakhal, Srimangal/Lawachara,
                  Tanguar Haor, Madhabkunda
      Khulna:     Sundarbans (Karamjal, Kotka), Sixty Dome Mosque/Bagerhat,
                  Mongla, Kuakata access points
      Barishal:   Kuakata, Durga Sagar, floating guava market (Bhimruli)
      Rajshahi:   Paharpur, Mahasthangarh, Puthia Temple Complex,
                  Varendra Museum, Padma riverside
      Rangpur:    Tajhat Palace, Kantaji Temple, Ramsagar,
                  Tetulia (Kanchenjunga view)
      Mymensingh: Birisiri/Durgapur, Shoshi Lodge, Gazni (Sherpur),
                  Muktagachha
    Per anchor: several hundred reviews, sorted by newest AND by most relevant,
    merged and de-duplicated. Prefer long reviews; do not filter to "top" only.

3.2 Snowball rounds
    Every entity found by the Phase 4 extractor with at least 2 independent
    mentions becomes a scrape target in the next round. Log round number,
    parent anchor and date for every place. "Discovery rounds until
    saturation" is part of the method section.

3.3 Bangla sources (where local knowledge actually lives)
    a) YouTube: comments (and auto-captions where available) on Bangla travel
       vlogs, collected through the official YouTube Data API v3. It is legal
       and free within quota, and rich in Banglish.
    b) Public Bangla travel blogs: fetch pages politely, extract article text
       with trafilatura, and respect robots.txt.
    c) TripAdvisor forum threads ("what else is near X?"), within its terms.
    NOT Facebook groups: scraping violates Meta's terms and involves private
    individuals' posts. This would damage the ethics review and a government
    sale.

3.4 Google Maps: how to scrape
    * Use an Apify reviews actor that returns BOTH original and translated
      text (field names like text/textTranslated or text/originalText; both
      are handled by add_reviews.py).
    * Run a Bangla pass (language "bn") as well as English. Google shows a
      review's original text reliably only when browsing in that language.
      Some actors sweep several languages and merge by review id; prefer
      those.
    * Use residential proxies. Zero returned reviews usually means a blocked
      datacenter IP.
    * Keep personal-data output OFF (no reviewer names or profile URLs).
    * Import:
        python scripts/add_reviews.py export.csv --source google_maps \
            --name gm_rajshahi_r1
      Check that the output says "kept original-language text for N rows".
      If it prints the "cannot be separated" warning, fix the actor settings
      and re-scrape. Do not import that batch.
    * Cost is roughly USD 0.5 per 1,000 reviews on pay-per-result actors, so
      10,000 reviews is a few dollars.

3.5 Schema additions (add in add_reviews.py / Phase 2 when first used)
      source_type   google_maps | booking | youtube | blog | tripadvisor
      collected_at  ISO timestamp of the scrape
      anchor_place  the famous place whose page the text came from
      discovery_round  0 for anchors, 1.. for snowball
      lat, lng      place coordinates (from the actor's place fields)
    Already added: review_text_original, original_language.

3.6 Place geography
    Extend data/place_geography.csv for every new place (district, division,
    lat, lng). Add a test that every place in processed_reviews.csv has a
    division.

Target: about 10,000 texts across all 8 divisions, at least 20% of them
Bangla or Banglish. The final size is decided by the saturation curve
(Phase 5), not by this number.

Done when: every division has 5+ anchors; 2+ snowball rounds; YouTube/blog
ingestion scripts committed with tests; corpus language mix reported.

4. PHASE 3 — THE LANGUAGE PIPELINE (weeks 3-6)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 Four-way language label, on review_text_original
      bn        Bangla script
      bn-latn   Banglish (romanised Bangla)       <- currently invisible
      en        English
      mixed     code-mixed (Bangla script + English, or Banglish + English)
    * Keep the existing Bengali-script regex.
    * Replace langdetect with fastText lid.176 for Latin-script text.
    * Banglish classifier: hand-label about 500 Latin-script texts (mostly
      from YouTube) as Banglish/English/mixed. Train a character 1-4-gram
      TF-IDF + logistic regression. Report its F1. The labelled set becomes
      part of the released dataset.
    * Keep data/language_overrides.csv for manual corrections.

4.2 Normalisation
    * Bangla Unicode normalisation with csebuetnlp/normalizer. It fixes
      inconsistent vowel signs and zero-width characters that silently break
      matching.
    * Banglish: phonetic back-transliteration (Avro-style rules) as an extra
      matching key only. Never replace the original text.

4.3 Embedding benchmark (a table for the paper)
    Candidates: paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2 (current), LaBSE,
    intfloat/multilingual-e5-base, BAAI/bge-m3.
    Test set: about 200 triples of (Bangla original, English translation,
    Banglish version), built from real reviews with Google's translations as
    the English side, plus hand-written Banglish.
    Metrics: cross-lingual retrieval accuracy@1 and @5 in both directions;
    encoding speed on CPU.
    Choose the model for Phases 3-4 and the product from this table, and
    re-run BERTopic with it.

4.4 Which text feeds which step
    Topic modelling: run on original text with the chosen multilingual model,
    and ALSO on the English analysis text. That comparison is an ablation
    (Phase 5).
    Entity extraction: always on the original text.

Done when: 4-way labels on the whole corpus; classifier F1 reported;
embedding table produced; the chosen model is used everywhere.

5. PHASE 4 — THE HIDDEN-GEM ENGINE (weeks 3-10)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.1 Annotation guidelines + gold set (start in week 3; it drives everything)
    Entity types:
      PLACE    a visitable location (beach stretch, temple, ghat, viewpoint)
      FOOD     a dish, sweet or eatery tied to the area (e.g. kotkoti)
      ACTIVITY a thing to do (boat ride, trek, market visit)
      ACCESS   a route or transport instruction
      TIP      practical advice (season, price, safety, bargaining)
    Write guidelines with examples and edge cases (generic "the beach" vs a
    named stretch; a hotel's own restaurant; nicknames).
    Double-annotate about 800 texts (current 538 + about 300 Bangla/Banglish).
    Report agreement (Cohen's kappa on tokens or span-level F1 between
    annotators). A small web tool or Label Studio (self-hosted, free) is fine.

5.2 Extraction
    Baseline: multilingual/Bangla NER (e.g. a BanglaBERT or XLM-R NER model).
    Main method: constrained LLM extraction (Gemini, as in Phase 4 today) with
      a JSON schema, and a HARD grounding rule: every entity must be an exact
      substring of the source text, or it is dropped. This keeps the
      project's no-hallucination guarantee and is measurable.
    Evaluate both against the gold set: span-level precision/recall/F1 per
    entity type and per language.

5.3 Entity resolution
    Cluster mentions that are the same real thing, using:
      * normalised string similarity (rapidfuzz) on original and
        back-transliterated forms
      * embedding similarity of the mention plus its sentence
      * geographic context (co-mentioned anchor, district)
    Evaluate on a hand-made set of variant groups (Guliakhali / Guliyakhali /
    Gulyakhali, Chor Bijoy / Char Bijoy, local vs official names). Report
    cluster purity and B-cubed F1.

5.4 Gazetteer and official visibility
    Sources: OpenStreetMap Bangladesh extract (Geofabrik, free), Wikidata,
    the BTB/district official attraction lists where published, and each
    candidate's Google Maps presence and review count.
    Visibility levels:
      V0 not on any map or list
      V1 on the map, few reviews
      V2 on the map, moderately reviewed
      V3 well known / official
    Locate V0 items via their anchor place and textual cues ("2 km south of
    the gate").

5.5 Gem score (transparent and ablatable, not a black box)
      mentions   independent mentions (distinct authors, sources, years)
      sentiment  mean sentiment of mention sentences
      hidden     inverse official visibility (V0 highest)
      distinct   semantic distinctiveness vs its anchor (reuse SDD)
      recency    share of mentions in the last 2-3 years
    Minimum 2-3 independent mentions before anything becomes a candidate.
    Weights are fixed in advance and reported, and every term is ablated in
    Phase 5.

5.6 Locals vs visitors
    Use reviewer_is_local_guide, user_location and language as proxies.
    Question: what do locals mention that visitors do not? Report
    distributions by entity type and division.

5.7 Verification queue (nothing auto-publishes)
    States: candidate -> verified | rejected | sensitive
    "sensitive" covers fragile ecology, restricted areas (e.g. parts of the
    Chittagong Hill Tracts needing permits) and private or home businesses.
    Sensitive items stay in the government register but never appear on the
    tourist side. Every decision records who, when and why.

Done when: gold set with agreement; extraction F1 table; resolution
evaluation; ranked candidate list with evidence sentences per division; the
queue works end to end.

6. PHASE 5 — RESEARCH RIGOUR (weeks 8-12)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

6.1 Baselines for gem discovery
      Popularity: rank places by review count (what maps do)
      LDA topics + keyword long tail
      BERTopic-only long tail (the current method)
      LLM-only: "List hidden gems in <district>" with no data. Measure how
        many named places cannot be found in any source or gazetteer
        (hallucination rate).
    Metric: precision@k (k = 10, 20) against human-verified gems, per
    division, plus coverage of V0/V1 items.

6.2 Ablations
      Original vs translated text: run extraction on review_text_original and
        on review_text, then count gems lost or mangled by translation (local
        names such as "Muradpur" are the typical casualties). Possible only
        because of the Phase 1 fix, and likely the most-cited table.
      Each gem-score term removed in turn.
      Embedding model choice (from 4.3).

6.3 Multiple seeds
    BERTopic and sensitivity analysis over 5-10 seeds; report mean and spread.
    The Phase 1 CRC32 fix makes each seed exactly reproducible.

6.4 Saturation curves
    New verified gems per additional 1,000 texts, per division. This answers
    "how much data is enough to map a region's hidden attractions?".
    Re-run the preference-taxonomy stability (Jaccard) on the large corpus
    too.

6.5 Re-run the original pipeline on the large corpus
    Phases 3-8 with the chosen embedding model. Report what happens to
    Direction-2 coverage (currently 3/5 weak) and topic-level long tail
    (currently 0), whichever way it goes.

6.6 Statistics
    Bootstrap confidence intervals on precision@k and F1. Significance tests
    between methods (paired bootstrap or McNemar).

Done when: every table in the paper outline (8.1) has numbers with intervals.

7. PHASE 6 — GOVERNMENT-GRADE PRODUCT (weeks 6-13)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

7.1 Data layer
    PostgreSQL + PostGIS (replaces SQLite). Keep build_phase8_database.py's
    rebuild-from-artifacts principle; add migrations (Alembic). Real
    coordinates allow real distances in the itinerary builder.

7.2 Incremental pipeline
    Fast path: a new batch gets topics via the saved BERTopic model's
      transform(), then gem extraction and resolution, then a DB upsert.
      (data/bertopic_model_dir/ is git-ignored, so store the model as a
      release artifact or on the server.)
    Scheduled full refit (e.g. monthly): produces a taxonomy DIFF (new,
      merged or dropped preferences) that an analyst approves before it goes
      live.
    Monitoring: counts per source, language mix and extraction volume per
      run, with alerts on sudden drops (a sign of a broken scraper).

7.3 Accounts, roles, audit
    Roles: public tourist; district officer (verifies gems in their district
    only); analyst (dashboards, exports); admin. Auth with hashed passwords or
    OAuth, and an audit log of every verification and taxonomy approval.

7.4 Government dashboard (the centre of the capstone demo)
    * Division/district map: gems by status, review volume, sentiment
    * District hidden-attraction register: candidates, verified, sensitive,
      with evidence
    * Visitor issues: aspect-based complaints (cleanliness, toilets, roads,
      safety, pricing, signage, harassment) per destination
    * Trends over time; demand vs supply by preference
    * One-click bilingual PDF brief per district
    * Data quality panel: coverage, language mix, last update

7.5 Tourist side
    Existing chat, itinerary and compare, now with verified gems ("Visiting
    Mahasthangarh? Locals also recommend..."). Every suggestion shows its
    evidence. Sensitive items never appear.

7.6 Bangla everywhere
    Bilingual UI (Bangla/English toggle). The chatbot gets Bangla and Banglish
    keyword lexicons plus an embedding-similarity intent fallback, so "Bogura
    te ki ki ache?" and "বগুড়ায় কী দেখার আছে?" both work. Keep it
    retrieval-only.

7.7 Hosting
    Paid Render instance (or a similar provider) for demo and judging; managed
    Postgres; nightly backups. Note for later: government buyers may require
    in-country hosting.

Done when: officer login -> verify a gem -> it appears for tourists; PDF brief
exports; Bangla queries work; all tests green.

10. ETHICS, LEGAL AND DATA MANAGEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * Collect only public content, and prefer official APIs (YouTube Data API).
    Respect robots.txt and rate limits. Record every source URL and scrape
    date.
  * No reviewer names, profile URLs or photos stored (personal-data output
    off).
  * Publish derived data and annotations, not raw platform text.
  * No Facebook group scraping.
  * The product sells insight, not review text. For a government deployment,
    propose first-party feedback collection (QR codes at sites, BTB forms)
    that the same pipeline can ingest legally.
  * Sensitive gems (fragile, restricted, private) are never shown publicly.
  * Update the ethics memo (Phase 0) with all of the above. The paper's
    ethics section summarises it.
