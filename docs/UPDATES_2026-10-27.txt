TOURISTINBD - WHAT CHANGED ON 27 OCTOBER 2026
===============================================

Written for the team and the supervisor. Plain language, no code. The work below
was done by Unaiza on top of the 15,567 Google Maps reviews Yakin collected.

In one paragraph
----------------
The hidden-gem engine now reads Bangla, understands what is a place and what is
a food or an activity, and no longer calls famous places "hidden". Before this
work it found no foods, no activities and nothing written in Bangla script, and
its number-one "gem" was the tip "Best Time to Visit". Now it finds 918 food
mentions, 2,037 activity mentions and 2,178 mentions written in Bangla, every one
traceable to the exact sentence in a real review. Each place has a card of what
it offers, a dozen local food specialities were found (Kushtia kulfi, Bogura
kotkoti, Sreemangal seven-layer tea...), and every gem now has a point on a map
and a label saying how recent its evidence is. No review was changed.

WHAT WAS DONE
=============

Part 1 - Places, foods and activities, in Bangla too
----------------------------------------------------

1. Only places are ranked as gems
   - Before, tips like "Best Time to Visit" and "visit early in the morning" were
     scored as if they were places, and the tip came first.
   - Now foods, activities, transport and tips are not gems. They describe the
     place the review is about, and appear on that place's card.

2. Foods and activities are found, from real review text
   - A reviewed word list (data/food_activity_lexicon.csv): about 45 foods and 20
     activities, each in English, Banglish and Bangla spellings ("fuchka",
     "ফুচকা"; "boat ride", "নৌকা ভ্রমণ").
   - Every spelling was checked by counting it in the reviews and reading the
     sentences before it was accepted.
   - Words that mislead are kept in the list as REJECTED, with the reason written
     down, so the decision can be checked:
       পান   means "get / drink" far more often than betel leaf
       আম    (mango) would match inside আমি ("I")
       ভেলা   (raft) is mostly the phrase মেঘের ভেলা, "a raft of clouds"
       crab   is mostly the red crabs on beaches, not food

3. Bangla names of places are recognised
   - 171 Bangla spellings were mined from the Bangla reviews themselves by matching
     their sound to known place names. 134 were accepted, 37 rejected with reasons
     (লোকাল "local" sounds like Lalakhal; পসরা "wares"; বর্ষার "of the monsoon").
     File: data/place_names_bn.csv.
   - Bangla word endings are handled: বান্দরবানের, আলুটিলায়, ইলিশের are found as
     বান্দরবান, আলুটিলা, ইলিশ. A word inside another word is never matched.
   - A Bangla review mentioning its own place (বান্দরবান in a Bandarban review) is
     not counted as a discovery, the same rule as for English.
   - Effect: Gangamati went from 6 to 12 supporting reviews, because half of its
     evidence was in Bangla and was invisible before.

4. Place cards and local specialities
   - For each of 104 places: the foods, activities, transport and tips its reviews
     mention, with counts and one real sentence for each
     (data/phase4_place_attributes.csv).
   - The 12 local specialities above (data/phase4_local_specialities.csv).

Part 2 - Famous places no longer called hidden; gems on the database and map
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

5. A serious error found and fixed: famous places were ranked as "undocumented"
   - Several "V0 - on no map" gems were places we had scraped from Google Maps
     ourselves: T-Badh (7,902 Google reviews), Bichnakandi (5,263, spelled
     "Bisnakandi"), Kantajew Temple (4,827, spelled "Kantaji Temple"), Mohamaya
     Lake, Karamjal, Bijoypur Lake, I-Badh. OpenStreetMap simply spells them
     differently.
   - The fix uses what the v2 plan already asks for in section 5.4: a candidate's
     Google Maps presence and review count. A place on our own place list is on a
     map, so it is never V0. Its level comes from its Google review count:
     1,000+ is V3, 100-999 is V2, fewer is V1.
   - This rule can only make a place LESS hidden, never more, so it removes false
     discoveries and cannot create new ones.
   - Spelling differences (Kantaji / Kantajew) are proposed automatically but only
     used once a person accepts them (data/place_spellings.csv: 5 accepted, each
     with a reason; one review even says "Kantaji Temple or Kantajew Temple").
   - 29 places moved up a level. 12 V0 places became 3.

6. How recent the evidence is
   - The reviews go back to 2011, so a place card could quote a 2017 bus fare.
   - Every gem now shows the year of its newest evidence and is marked "recent"
     (a review in the last 3 years) or "old". Old gems stay listed, because the
     evidence is real, but are flagged "needs recent confirmation". Today: 3 of 54
     (Hiron Point, Puthia, I-Badh).
   - Place cards quote a recent review whenever one exists, and transport or tips
     with no recent review are marked out of date. 326 of the 1,064 card rows
     have no review from the last 3 years.
   - The 3-year window is the same one the gem score already used, kept in one
     place so the two can never disagree.
   - Checked on the data: 89% of reviews are from 2018 or later and only 11 are
     older than 2016. The Bangla share has been steady at about 25% every year
     since 2019, so it does not drift with time.

7. Gems, place cards and specialities are in the database and the API
   - Database version 3: three new tables (gems, place_attributes,
     local_specialities), map coordinates for the 100 listed places, and two
     views that work out freshness automatically.
   - New API endpoints (all read-only):
       /api/gems                 ranked gems; filter by level, division, freshness
       /api/gems/map             map data: gem points and the 100 places
       /api/gems/{id}            one gem with its sentences, newest first
       /api/place-attributes     what a place offers, with real sentences
       /api/local-specialities   the specialities
   - Every map point says where it came from: OpenStreetMap (36), our Google Maps
     place list (15), or - for the 3 places on no map at all - "near" the place
     whose reviews mention them (Gangamati and Fatrar Chor near Kuakata).
   - Madhabpur Lake has no point: placing it "near" the review that mentions it
     would have put it in the wrong district, so it is left off and listed as
     unmapped instead.

8. Other mistakes found and fixed along the way
   - The name merger crashed on a Bangla/English pair; fixed.
   - Bangla word endings with য় failed to match because of two ways of writing
     the same letter; fixed.
   - Seven duplicate entities were merged (for example "Padma" had two IDs). Their
     old IDs are kept as pointers, so nothing that refers to them breaks.

Part 3 - The gem map on the website
-----------------------------------
Before: the only way to see gems on the website was a table in the Discoveries
tab (docs/screenshots/2026-10-27_before_gems_only_as_a_table.png).
After: a new "Gem map" tab (docs/screenshots/2026-10-27_after_gem_map.png and
2026-10-27_after_gem_evidence.png).

9. What the map shows
   - Every gem with a point, coloured by how well known it already is: V0 (on no
     map) pink, V1 orange, V2 blue, V3 grey. Bigger point = higher gem score.
   - The 100 places our reviews were written about, as small grey dots, so you
     can see that the gems lie AROUND the famous places, not on them.
   - A dashed ring marks an approximate point (a V0 place drawn near the place
     whose reviews mention it). Two gems at the same approximate spot are fanned
     out slightly so neither hides the other.
   - A faded point has no review from the last 3 years.
   - Filters: by level (V3 off by default, so famous places do not crowd the
     map), reviewed places on/off, recent evidence only.
   - The one gem with no coordinates anywhere (Madhabpur Lake) is listed under
     the map instead of being placed somewhere wrong.

10. Clicking a gem opens its evidence
   - Its level and why, whether its evidence is recent, the five parts of its
     score as bars, which places' reviews mention it, every spelling seen
     (Gangamati: গঙ্গামতি, Gangamati, গঙ্গামাটি), and the reviewers' own
     sentences, newest first, in Bangla and English.

11. Direct links for the demo
   - /app/#map opens the map; /app/#map/E0016 opens it on Gangamati's evidence.
     Any tab works the same way (/app/#discoveries).

12. How it is built
   - Leaflet 1.9.4 with OpenStreetMap tiles, both free. The library is pinned to
     an exact version and checked against its published fingerprint, so a
     changed file from the internet is refused. If it cannot load, the tab says
     so instead of showing a blank box.
   - The map makes one request (/api/gems/map); the filters work on that copy.
   - The website is now light-themed only; the dark mode switch was removed.
   - 44 new checks in the website contract tests (149 now): the tab, the pinned
     library, and every field the map reads is really returned by the API.


Part 4 - Place cards: what each place offers
--------------------------------------------
Before: opening a place showed its rating, its preference tags and a few
reviews (docs/screenshots/2026-10-27_before_place_view.png).
After: the same place also shows what it offers
(docs/screenshots/2026-10-27_after_place_card.png).

13. The place card
   - Four groups: Food to try, Things to do, Getting there and around, Tips from
     visitors. Each item says how many reviews mention it and in which years, and
     quotes one real sentence with the year it was written. Recent reviews are
     quoted whenever there is one.
   - Transport and tips with no review in the last 3 years are marked "may be
     out of date - last mentioned <year>". Food and activities are not, since a
     2018 mention of kulfi is still useful; a 2018 bus fare may not be.
   - A local speciality is highlighted: "Local speciality: Kulfi - 10 of 10
     reviews that mention it are from Kushtia".
   - Long lists show 5 items and a "Show more".

14. Where it appears
   - Explore: search, then click a place. A strip of the 12 local specialities
     sits at the top of Explore; each opens the place they come from.
   - Gem map: click a grey place dot, then "What it offers".
   - Direct link: /app/#explore/Kuakata Sea Beach.

15. Honest limits, visible on the cards
   - The food and activity list matches words, not meaning. "Mango" at the
     Shilaidaha Kuthibari is the mango garden, not something to eat, and its
     "speedboat ride" is Tagore's speedboat on display. Every item shows its
     sentence, so a reader can see this, but the list is not a guarantee.
   - Tips come from the older tip extractor and repeat themselves ("Bargaining",
     "bargain", "bargain for a reasonable price" are separate items).
   - 20 new website checks (169 now), including that a place name with an
     apostrophe ("Cox's Bazar") cannot break a button.


Part 5 - What translation loses (a measured result)
---------------------------------------------------
The project's rule is to extract from the reviewer's own words, never from a
translation. This part measures why, on our own data.

16. The experiment
   - Google Maps gave 285 Bangla reviews (and 10 Banglish ones) in two versions:
     what the reviewer wrote, and Google's English translation of it. Same
     review, same person, same day, so translation is the only difference.
   - The same extractor was run over both versions, and every place, food,
     activity and transport mode was checked: found in the original, in the
     translation, or both? (Tips are left out: their patterns are English-only,
     so they could only ever fire on the translation, which would be unfair.)
   - Program: scripts/translation_loss.py. Full write-up:
     data/translation_loss_report.md. Every pair: data/translation_loss_pairs.csv.

17. Results (after the Bangla name list was completed in Part 6)
   - Of what the original names, the translation still names 69%
     (95% interval 60-77%, bootstrap over reviews). Nearly a third is lost.
   - Places: 58 of 84 survive translation (69%).
       কটকা              -> "Katka"           (the map knows it as Kotka)
       সাদা মাটির পাহাড়    -> "the white clay hills"
       বিজয়পুর            -> "Vijaypur"        (the map knows Bijoypur)
       চিনা মাটির পাহাড়    -> "Chinese clay hill"
   - Even if every respelled name were recovered by fuzzy matching, at most 83%
     would survive. The rest were turned into descriptions or dropped.
   - Banglish: Google kept the meaning of 0 of 10. Examples:
       "sei gorom" ("so hot")                 -> "six o'clock"
       "Onek onek sundor" ("very beautiful")   -> "One piece one piece"
       "Ekhane giye akta fuchka kine
        kheyechilam..." (bought a fuchka here) -> "I've been working hard for a
                                                  long time..." (fuchka gone)
     The 10 judgements are in data/banglish_translation_check.csv, each with a
     reason, and can be changed there. (One person's judgement.)
   - Why the number moved: the first run (50%) was partly measuring gaps in
     our own Bangla name list. Once সুন্দরবন, কুয়াকাটা, জাফলং and 16 others
     were added (Part 6), more places were found in the originals and the
     comparison became fairer. 69% is the better estimate; both are recorded.

18. It cuts both ways, and the report says so
   - Our Bangla name list still misses 15 place mentions that the translation
     catches (it missed 49 before Part 6). All 15 are well-known places.
   - Which side misses what: of the places translation lost, 41% were little
     known (V0-V1); of the places our Bangla list missed, 0%. Translation tends
     to destroy exactly the local names the gem engine is looking for.
   - Honest status: Fisher exact test p = 0.066. Close, but not below 0.05 on
     285 reviews: a strong direction, not yet a proven result. More Bangla
     data (e.g. YouTube comments) is what would settle it.

19. Where to see it
   - Website: Overview tab, "Lost in translation" panel
     (docs/screenshots/2026-10-27_after_lost_in_translation.png).
   - API: /api/research/translation-loss.
   - 27 new tests (data pairing, cross-script fairness, the Fisher test checked
     against a textbook value, reproducible bootstrap, recorded judgements never
     overwritten) plus 4 API checks.


Part 6 - Places talked about together, blind spots, and more Bangla names
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

20. More Bangla place names (data/place_names_bn.csv: 134 -> 153 accepted)
   - The name finder matched by sound, and missed names whose English spelling
     has letters Bangla does not write: Sundarbans vs সুন্দরবন (the plural s),
     Jaflong vs জাফলং, Mahasthangarh vs মহাস্থানগড়. It now also compares a
     looser version of the sound (used only for finding names, so the
     name-merging score, F1 0.966, is unchanged).
   - A second source: in reviews Google gave in two versions, the English
     translation says which place the Bangla review is about. That is how
     কুয়াকাটা (Kuakata) and কক্সবাজার (Cox's Bazar) were found.
   - 47 new candidates were checked against real sentences: 19 accepted, 28
     rejected with a reason, e.g. মান্নানলাল is Mannanlal Roy (a person, not
     Mongla), "সময়ও লাগে" means "it also takes time", "কয়েকটি" means "several".
     শশী লজ (Shashi Lodge) was added by hand from a review.
   - Word endings added so names are found inside sentences: সহ ("with"),
     ঃ typed as a colon, and কেও ("…too").
   - Effect: mentions 6,938 -> 7,509 (+571), all still traced to exact text.

21. Places talked about together (data/phase4_place_connections_report.md)
   - 167 links between 104 places: a place mentioned inside a review of
     another, or two places named in the same review, each in at least 2
     reviews, each with a real sentence.
   - The links that lead to lesser-known places are the gem engine's idea made
     visible: Gangamati in 8 Kuakata reviews and beside the Sundarbans in 7;
     Birisiri with Bijoypur Lake (13); Muradpur with Guliakhali (24, "locals
     call this beach Muradpur beach").
   - On the website: the Gem map draws these links as purple lines, and a
     gem's evidence lists what it is "talked about together with".

22. A false gem caught: Pashur (rank #4)
   - The links showed "trees like Shundari, Pashur, Kewra, Golpata". পশুর is
     a Sundarbans mangrove tree as well as a river. 5 of Pashur's 10 reviews
     mean the tree. Not fixed silently: this is exactly what the human
     verification queue is for, and it should be rejected or split there.

23. Blind spots: where the engine cannot see (data/coverage_report.md)
   - 32 of Bangladesh's 64 districts have no reviewed place at all; 13 more
     are thin (under 100 reviews or one place); 19 are covered.
   - Worst divisions: Dhaka (9 of 13 districts empty), Rangpur (6 of 8),
     Khulna (5 of 10).
   - This is the case for the next collection round (YouTube, more places):
     a district with no data is not a district without gems.
   - On the website: a "Blind spots" panel under the Gem map, every district
     coloured no data / thin / covered.
   - New list: data/bd_districts.csv (all 64 districts and their divisions).

24. Small fix: links like /app/#Map now work as well as /app/#map.
   - 31 new tests (connections, coverage, wider name mining), 8 API checks and
     24 website checks.

25. Search fixed (reported: "bamboo chicken" in Explore showed nothing useful)
   - Case was never the problem: "bamboo chicken" and "BAMBOO CHICKEN" always
     gave the same results. The real problems were:
       * search did not know about foods, activities or gems at all;
       * each review was shown by its first sentence, so the matched words
         were usually not visible;
       * review results could not be clicked;
       * a real Bangla bug: the letter য় (and ড়, ঢ়) can be stored two ways
         in Unicode, and reviews use both. Typing কুয়াকাটা found 19 or 30
         reviews depending on the keyboard; it now finds all 47.
   - Now: foods, activities, transport and gems are searchable by any spelling
     (English, Banglish or Bangla); words match in any order; matched words
     are highlighted in a snippet around them; every result opens something:
     a place card, the place a review is about, the gem on the map, or for a
     food, every place whose reviews mention it with a real sentence
     ("Bamboo chicken - a local speciality of Rangamati - Sajek Valley,
     Boga Lake").
   - Screenshots: docs/screenshots/2026-10-27_before_search_bamboo_chicken.png
     and 2026-10-27_after_search_bamboo_chicken.png.
   - 21 new checks. 1,554 tests in 26 suites now.
   - Screenshot: docs/screenshots/2026-10-27_after_connections_and_blind_spots.png
